Indexing standard for the Central Records Repository and SharePoint

Every scanned document gets a predictable file name and matching metadata so it can be found by any of four routes: student, school, document type, or year.

File name pattern: StudentID_LastName_DocType_SchoolCode_YYYY-MM-DD
Example: 2009471_RIVERA_PermRecord_412_2019-06-14.pdf

SharePoint metadata columns: Student ID (indexed), Last name, First name, Birth date, Document type (choice), School code (lookup), School year, Retention category (choice), Retention until (calculated), Confidential flag.

Document type codes: PermRecord, Transcript, Withdrawal, Immunization, Diploma, IEP-Archive, Discipline, Correspondence.

Why it works: staff can search by ID when they have it, and fall back to name plus birth date when they don’t; the retention column makes the yearly disposition review a filtered view instead of a manual hunt.

Verifying identity and authorization before release
  1. Confirm the requester’s identity: government ID for walk-ins; matching name, birth date and last school for online requests.
  2. Check who has rights to the record: the parent until the student turns 18 or enrolls in college, then the eligible student.
  3. Confirm a signed release is on file unless a FERPA exception applies (school transfer, health or safety emergency, valid subpoena after notice).
  4. Record the exception or release in the tracker before producing anything.
  5. Log every disclosure: who received it, what was sent, when, and why.
Compiling complete subpoena and transcript information

A single system rarely holds the whole record, so I check each source in order and note what was found:

  1. Current student information system (enrollment, grades, attendance).
  2. Prior or legacy SIS exports for older years.
  3. Central Records Repository scans (permanent record cards, withdrawals).
  4. SharePoint archives and school-submitted files.
  5. Paper back-files in storage, pulled and scanned if needed.

A cover sheet lists each source checked and the result, so the supervisor can sign off knowing the search was complete.

Efficiency improvement: same-day transcript counter

Problem (sample scenario): walk-in transcript requests were queued with mailed requests, averaging 4 days.

Recommendation: a pre-verified intake checklist at the counter, a dedicated print queue, and a daily 2–3 pm walk-in block.

Measure: track walk-in turnaround in the tracker; target is same-day completion for complete records.

Hardware and software installation checklist
  1. Confirm request and approval; check the device meets software requirements.
  2. Coordinate with OIT for admin rights, licenses and network access.
  3. Back up local settings and data; schedule the install outside counter hours.
  4. Install, update, and connect to the SIS, printers and scanners.
  5. Test with sample (non-production) records.
  6. Record asset tag, version and date in the resource tracker and change log.
  7. Show the user what changed; write a one-page quick guide if needed.
Daily network and system monitoring
  • Confirm SIS, SharePoint and the repository are reachable at start of day.
  • Check shared printers and scanners are online with queues clear.
  • Verify overnight scan uploads finished and file counts match.
  • Review the help desk queue for open records-office tickets.
  • Log outages with time, impact and ticket number; notify staff and requesters if turnaround is affected.

How it would fit City Schools

The system is designed to work alongside Scribbles, not replace it. Scribbles continues to handle online transcript ordering, while the SharePoint tracker covers what sits around it: subpoenas and their legal review, communications with schools and agencies, equipment, and one dashboard across all of it. In a real rollout I would start by learning the office’s current process and adapt the lists and flows to match, coordinate with the Office of Information Technology on permissions, and restrict subpoena records to records staff and Legal.

Public service and communication

Sample responses to the range of people a records office serves, and how I handle situations that need extra tact.

Response to a college: transcript sent
Dear Admissions Office, The official transcript for [Student Name], requested on [date], was sent electronically today through our secure delivery service. Please contact our office at [phone] or [email] if you have trouble opening it or need an additional copy. Sincerely, [Your Name], Records Management
Response to a law firm: subpoena acknowledgment
Dear [Attorney Name], We received your subpoena for records of [Student Name] on [date]. It has been forwarded to the Office of Legal Counsel for review, as required by district policy and federal law. You will be contacted by that office regarding next steps. Records will not be released before that review is complete. Sincerely, [Your Name], Records Management
Response to a former student: missing information
Hello [Name], Thank you for your transcript request. To find your record, we need your date of birth and the name of the last school you attended. Once we have these, your request will be completed within five business days. You can reply to this email or call us at [phone]. Thank you, [Your Name], Records Management
Situations requiring unusual tact

A parent without legal access asks for a child’s records. I explain calmly that I can’t release records without confirming custody rights, tell them exactly which document would allow access, and offer to check as soon as they bring it. I never confirm or deny details of the record in the meantime.

A graduate needs a transcript today for a job offer, but a grade is missing. I call the school registrar while they wait, and if the grade can’t be confirmed I offer an enrollment verification letter immediately plus a priority transcript once the record is complete.

A record cannot be found. I explain the search steps I’ve taken, the sources still to check, and when I will follow up, then log the commitment in the tracker so it happens.
